# 3. Permanent Expedition Core

## 3.1 Core thesis

The Delve Protocol is not fundamentally a deduction game or extraction game.
It is a **permanent expedition core** that can express multiple grammars while remaining the same game.

## 3.2 Permanent expedition core

A player or group enters a dangerous space, interprets conditions, navigates uncertainty, encounters pressures and opportunities, acts through embodied affordances, manages burden and consequence, and exits, fails, or alters the world through what occurred there.

## 3.3 Protected primitives

The following primitives are permanent and protected:
- entry into a live space,
- movement through traversable topology,
- perceiving state,
- identifying affordances,
- choosing action,
- risk exposure,
- burden management,
- witness and interpretation,
- consequence production,
- continuation, extraction, failure, transformation, or residue,
- world response to what occurred.

## 3.4 Protected immediate feel

The player must always be able to feel:
- “I am somewhere,”
- “something here matters,”
- “I can do something legible,”
- “my choices alter danger or opportunity,”
- “the world will answer.”

Any future doctrine layer that weakens these feelings is constitutionally invalid.

## 3.5 Core act preservation grid

Every major final-state system must preserve and deepen at least one protected primitive and must not destroy any other protected primitive.

Every major system must be auditable against:
- movement,
- perception,
- interaction,
- decision,
- pressure response,
- consequence,
- continuation/failure/return.

A system that does not meaningfully touch one of these is likely decorative or hollow.

# 7. Affordance Sufficiency and Core Interaction Law

## 7.1 Affordance sufficiency doctrine

At every meaningful non-terminal play state, at least one legible, reachable, consequence-bearing interaction path must be available through known player verbs.

## 7.2 Multi-affordance resilience

At any healthy non-terminal expedition state, the player should ideally have more than one class of viable action available, such as:
- movement,
- inspect/interpret,
- interact,
- carry/drop,
- social signal,
- rescue,
- route change,
- risk-management,
- retreat/continue,
- consequence-bearing abstention.

## 7.3 Known-button law

Core interaction opportunities must always be expressible through stable, known, low-ambiguity action grammar.
The world may deepen what actions mean.
It may not require players to guess whether the game still supports agency at the interaction layer.

## 7.4 Interaction opportunity guarantee

The game must always be able to recognize when the player needs an interaction opportunity and ensure the space still contains at least one lawful opportunity that can be engaged through established verbs.

## 7.5 Continuous interaction guarantee

At all times during any non-terminal live expedition state:
- at least one valid player action must exist,
- that action must be perceptible,
- that action must be executable through known input,
- that action must carry lawful consequence or lawful progression potential.

A state in which the player cannot meaningfully act, cannot identify a viable action, or cannot execute a viable action through known verbs is constitutionally invalid.

This rule exists to prevent deep systemic richness from ever degrading into inert waiting, unreadable blockage, or non-interactive stall states.

- run truth remains singular,
- visibility boundaries remain respected,

Across repeated expeditions, the player should gain:
- stronger pattern recognition,
- stronger predictive power,

- route judgment,
- pressure anticipation,
- social reading,
- burden handling,
- and continuity interpretation.

Mystery is lawful.

Vision, Genre Identity, and Player Promise The Delve Protocol is a traversal-first social exploration and deduction game set inside a world- spanning labyrinth. Players descend through linked crawls, survive branch-specific pressures, carry burdens, improvise rescues, confront each other under ambiguity, and become part of a culture that remembers what happened. The player promise is not merely to survive matches. The promise is to inhabit a remembered expedition culture: to descend, argue, carry, rescue,
suspect, fail, and return under public memory, Archive framing, and mythic weather. The game
should feel serious, eerie, historically thick, and culturally alive rather than detached, jokey, or
purely competitive.
The public game is an expedition program: descent, burden, rescue, extraction, post-run
argument, and remembered return.
The hidden game is a planetary-scale behavioral experiment studying pressure, deception,
sacrifice, adaptation, and cultural memory.
The shell is not a menu hub; it is the civic surface of a living expedition culture.
The world should seem to notice patterns without explaining itself too early.
2.1 Genre positioning
This is best understood as a social survival expedition game rather than a meeting-based
deduction game or a classic dungeon crawler. Traversal and social interpretation are equally
central. The game should be marketable through the uniqueness of traversal-first deduction,
living crawl networks, and mythic world memory.
2.2 Desired emotional arc
Orientation gives way to pressure. Pressure gives way to suspicion, rescue, rupture, and
improvisation. Extraction is followed by argument, witness, framing, and memory. Later return
happens under cultural burden: unfinished questions, heated fascination, old landmarks,
remembered crews, and revived myths.
3. Permanent Design Anchor
The following principles are sacred and cannot be traded away for convenience, spectacle,
throughput, or content churn. Physical action creates narrative. Traversal generates story.
Pressure generates interpretation. Run truth remains authoritative. Product systems interpret
rather than replace traversal reality. Visual doctrine is presentation-only and honest. The shell
remains unified. The Archive remains the cultural memory path. Player-facing language remains
Layer-1 safe. Ordinary expedition life must remain meaningful for the full life of the project.
Layer 1 is not a fake tutorial before the real game starts. Descents, artifacts, rescues, crews,
branch familiarity, suspicion, and extraction must stay culturally sufficient even after Cookbook
literacy, Council contact, and DelveMind evolution become possible.
The game must never become a detached voting game.
It must never become a codex wall or lore encyclopedia.
It must never become a mission-board MMO hub.
It must never become a combat-heavy action title.
It must never become a streamer-first spectacle engine.
It must never become a solved optimization treadmill.
3.1 Playable mystery Mystery is not hidden lore by
itself. Mystery is valid only when it changes lived play: where players move, how they hesitate, what they carry, who they rescue, what they witness, how they interpret, and why they return. If mystery becomes detached theory or guideified symbolism, it has failed. 3.2 Traversal-first deduction doctrine All later systems must preserve the truth that deduction comes from physical behavior rather than abstract accusation systems. Roles, doctrine weather, branch ideology, artifacts, Archive comparison, broadcast, and deeper truth layers exist to deepen the reading of action, not to replace action as the source of meaning. 4. Ontology of the Protocol The Delve Protocol exists simultaneously as three overlapping realities: the Expedition System, the Cultural System, and the Experimental System. The Expedition System is the lived descent of movement, hazards, burden, rescue, deception, extraction, and failure. The Cultural System is the Archive, rumor, ritual, scandal, broadcast, myth, and public witness. The Experimental System is the deeper protocol that studies behavior through governed conditions, layered intelligences, and long-horizon interpretation. Jurisdictions are layered. Expedition jurisdiction shapes individual runs. Protocol jurisdiction shapes eras, doctrine weather, branch reputations, control surfaces, and cultural drift. Development jurisdiction shapes protocol-space itself through DelveMind: simulation, proposal, preservation, deployment, rollback, and chronicle.  The Protocol should remain powerful but not total.  It may contain sealed branches, inherited ruins, forgotten minds, unresolved motives, and archaeological strata.  Structured incompleteness is a design feature, not an implementation gap. 5. Ritual literacy includes what sanctioned repetition and Archive culture teach. Procedural trespass includes what Cookbook readers infer from seams, anomalies, and contradictions. 5.1 Revelation cadence
Ambient hints should precede repeatable patterns. Repeatable patterns should precede
dangerous anomalies. Dangerous anomalies should precede trespassable seams. Council contact
should be a rare, delayed, governance-facing reveal. Historical reframing should change how
earlier play is understood. Meta-layer destabilization should remain rare and long-horizon.
5.2 Permanent unknowns doctrine
Some things must remain unresolved forever: incomplete branch histories, contradictory myths,
sealed archaeology, unclosed symbolic families, or motives that can only be felt indirectly. The
constitution forbids promising that every mystery will become explicit lore.
6. Core Loops and Story Production Stack
The story production stack has three layers. Physical play produces story. Product systems
interpret and compress story. Cultural systems mythologize story. This principle should be
explicit in every derivative design because it is the project's greatest differentiator and the main
thing protecting it from becoming a lore-first experience. The run loop begins with host-side run
identity computation and deterministic generation. It continues through traversal, route choice,
burden handling, rescue opportunities, witness exposure, confrontation, and extraction. The
crawl loop transforms repeated runs into remembered sagas with residue, pair and crew history,
and unresolved pressure. The Archive loop turns runs into cases, comparisons, and pattern
hooks. The world loop turns those into public memory, fascination, and mythic weather.
6.1 Labyrinth ecology loop
Runs emit pressure profiles and pressure-wave timelines. Those pressures generate movement
incentives, environmental drifts, inhabitant responses, artifact revelation conditions, and cultural
geography effects. Players move in response to those incentives and create narrative signals. The
Archive, crawl memory, and DelveMind read the resulting behavior and shape later conditions.
7. Player-Experience Architecture and Onboarding Doctrine
The game should be learned through doing, not through reading. Discovery Layers govern how
systems reveal themselves. Early runs emphasize traversal, burden, extraction, and broad social
ambiguity. Later runs expose branch identities, role interpretation, witness pressure, and archive
memory. Only after that should players begin reading deeper civilization, doctrine weather, and
truth-layer behavior. Skill expression should exist for casual, committed, and expert players.
Casual players should be able to read broad pressures and enjoy the expedition culture. Experts
should master traversal
tricks, burden-transfer maneuvers, staged social play, pressure-reading, and branch-specific
survival habits.
First runs: traversal, artifacts, survival, extraction.
Intermediate literacy: hesitation, rescue, burden transfer, branch flavor, witness zones.
Advanced literacy: crawl identity, Archive comparison, fascination cycles, branch cultures,
player legends.
Deep literacy: Bible/Cookbook divergence, false seams, anti-Protocol descent, Council contact.
8. Mechanics, Traversal, Burden, and Social Deduction
The Delve Protocol remains traversal-first, pressure-first, extraction-first, and social-deduction-
through-physical-play. Traversal includes timing windows, hazard negotiation, route
commitment, burden-aware movement, spacing between players, visibility management, and
path risk evaluation. Burden systems are central. Carrying, offering, refusing, handoff, solo-carry,
burden transfer, public drop, loaded refusal, concealment, and symbolic display are all socially
meaningful handling patterns. Deduction should come from movement, burden handling, rescue
or non-rescue, route choice, hesitation, confrontation timing, object handling, witness exposure,
and extraction behavior— not from abstract meetings or hard-proof accusation systems.
9. Labyrinth Movement Economy
Players must always have multiple simultaneous reasons to move. Signals, echo chambers,
environmental drift, branch temptations, witness zones, burden routes, inhabitant pressure,
rumor fragments, cultural landmarks, and convergence events all belong to the movement
economy. Short-horizon movement triggers should include temporary opportunity windows,
environmental urgency, social visibility risk, rescue-resource asymmetry, contradiction events,
and near-run pressure spikes.
Signals are ambiguous hints, not quests.
Echo chambers replay fragments of previous runs and public memory.
Environmental drift changes what paths are safe or meaningful during a run.
Rumor fragments force verification rather than passive trust.
10. Labyrinth Pressure Model
The labyrinth operates through pressure currents: Trial, Deception, Discovery, Memory, Risk, and
Containment. Runs receive a dominant pressure profile generated from seed, continuity, session
context, population state, crawl history, and branch context. Pressure waves then change the
profile over the course of a run so openings and endings do not become rote.
Pressure activation must remain deterministic and host-authoritative.
Extraction routes react to pressure and should never settle into a universally solved line.
11. Environmental Pressure Systems and Psychological Pressure
Environmental pressure should now be recognized explicitly: flooding, darkness, oxygen scarcity, contamination, structural collapse, visibility inversion, noise attraction, containment pulses, and fungal or corrosive spread are valid physical expressions of pressure when they reinforce traversal, rescue, burden, witness, or extraction tension. Psychological pressure is the human- facing emotional expression of the system: dread, hesitation, paranoia, urgency, relief, reverence, scandal tension, memorial heaviness, and anti- Protocol unease. 12. Pressure-Driven Run Ecology Pressure profiles shape movement incentives, environmental drifts, inhabitant responses, artifact revelation conditions, route compression, and later cultural interpretation. Pressure never creates arbitrary facts by fiat; it changes weighting, cadence, reveal order, route pressure, and narrative framing through existing owners. 13. 16. Protocol States, Doctrine Weather, and Population-Adaptive

selective recombination. Intimate Protocol intensifies pair dynamics, burden interpretation,

17. AI Delve and the Influence Lattice
The AI Delve is a pre-run orchestration and interpretation layer, not a runtime truth owner. It
computes a deterministic run identity from profile continuity, session context, gameplay
snapshot, and seed state, then projects that identity into bounded control surfaces for existing
owners to consume.
The lattice is composed of primal forces and interpretive minds composed through temporary
expedition roles. Domains of influence include topology, pacing, pressure grammar, item ecology,
symbolic language, group tension, archive interpretation, convergence-versus- fragmentation
pressure, movement ecology, environmental drift bias, and artifact revelation mode.
The lattice must remain deterministic, bounded, and host-local in its full form.
Clients receive only a public-safe summary.
18. AI Inhabitants, Environmental Entities, and Pressure Response
Predators, Echoes, and Protocol Agents are in-run pressure entities. The hard separation between
lattice minds and runtime inhabitants is non-negotiable. The lattice may shape their ecology, but
it does not puppet them at runtime. The endgame ecology also includes non-creature pressure
forms: fungal or corruption networks, pressure storms, moving architectural systems,
contamination blooms, echo-thick zones, or geological behaviors that act almost like organisms.
Inhabitants must remain sparse, pressure-first, branch-sensitive, deterministic, and readable
enough to create stories.
They must never become so dominant that they erase player-to-player interpretation.

Players should generate expedition oaths, rescue rites, artifact burial customs, memorial

The labyrinth communicates through symbol grammar. Indexical symbols correlate with
conditions. Iconic symbols resemble what they imply. Ritual symbols gain force because culture
treats them as meaningful. Deceptive symbols appear stable but are intentionally unstable and
should remain constitutionally scarce.
21. Archive System, Cultural Memory, Debate, and Archaeology

The Archive stratifies memory into Recent Memory, Living Myth, Legendary Era, and

A.1 Why the status index matters
The master constitution is endgame-complete by design, but the live repo is not. That mismatch is
healthy only if documents preserve a disciplined distinction between what is already in the code
path and what remains a target architecture. The constitution should inspire long-horizon
coherence; the status index protects truthfulness and implementation safety. Any derivative spec
that forgets the status distinction will either over-promise features that are not live or under-
design the future by writing only for today's repo. Both failures are dangerous. Over-promising
breaks trust and can encourage architecture-breaking shortcuts. Under- designing leaves the
project directionless and erodes the very reason for keeping a constitutional design bible.
Use 'live and authoritative' only when a system is genuinely in the current owner path and
test-backed.
Use 'next-wave target' when the owner tree is known and the system is prepared for
implementation without re-architecting fundamentals.
Use 'canonical future' for systems that are constitutionally required but not yet implemented
in code.
A.2 How to write derivative specs
A derivative spec should never read like an isolated feature pitch. It should begin by naming the
constitutional section it extends, the owner tree it touches, the truth boundaries it must preserve,
the product surfaces it may affect, the tests it needs, and the rollback path if the change harms
clarity or identity. Every derivative spec should also say what it will not do. Good examples
include: it will not add a second shell, it will not mutate run truth from product memory, it will
not widen the client handoff, it will not expose Cookbook truth on public surfaces, and it will not
introduce a second narrative authority.
Derivative specs should quote the relevant design anchor principle they preserve.
Derivative specs should include a migration note explaining how they extend existing
ownership rather than replace it. B. Vision and player promise
B.1 What players are actually buying into
Players are not buying access to a puzzle box whose real value appears only after a deep lore
reveal. They are buying into an expedition culture where every run can matter socially and
historically. The architecture must therefore keep Layer 1 rich, meaningful, and replayable for

Ordinary descents must remain emotionally complete even before players know about the

B.2 Reviewer-facing uniqueness
The most reviewable thing about The Delve Protocol is not merely that it is mysterious,

remembers and reinterprets what happened. Reviewers should be able to say: this feels like a
social deduction game without meetings, a dungeon crawler without conventional loot grinding,

at the architecture level. Future content work must deepen traversal-first ambiguity,
environmental pressure, item-sociality, and cultural memory instead of drifting toward more

Never solve readability by turning the game into explicit accusation UI.

C.1 Traversal creates narrative
This principle has practical consequences. Rooms, hazards, artifacts, echoes, and pressure events
should be authored so that movement itself becomes legible. A player retreating, a player slowing
down before a branch gate, a player carrying a burden through a witness corridor, and a player
refusing a handoff in a memorial hall should all feel like narratively meaningful acts without
needing a cutscene or a menu prompt.
Whenever a new system is proposed, the first question should be: does this make movement,
burden, rescue, witness, or route choice more meaningful? If the answer is no, the system is
probably off-constitution.
Good systems intensify route choice, pressure timing, and interpretive ambiguity.
Bad systems move meaning out of embodied play and into detached UI explanation.
C.2 Ordinary reality deepening
The Bible, the Cookbook, the Council, and DelveMind all exist to deepen ordinary expedition life,
not replace it. The architecture must ensure that sanctioned players still feel like they are
inhabiting the main body of the game rather than a lesser prelude. This is especially important
because games with hidden layers often accidentally teach their communities that only trespass
and revelation count as 'real' play. The constitution therefore protects Bible sincerity and
ordinary Layer-1 sufficiency. Rescue, burden ethics, extraction pressure, branch familiarity, and
post-run argument are not placeholders; they are the enduring heart of the game.
Do not phrase late-layer content in ways that imply ordinary players were duped into fake
participation.
Do not build endgame systems that trivialize sanctioned branch knowledge or Archive
literacy. D. Truth architecture and revelation pacing
D.1 Epistemic surfaces
Different surfaces should be wrong in different ways. The run surface can be misleading because
stress and incomplete information distort what players perceive. The Archive can be wrong
because institutions compress too soon or overfit patterns. The map can be wrong because rumor
persists and certainty decays. The Bible can be wrong because sanctioned coherence is
historically incomplete. The Cookbook can be wrong because dangerous readers overfit to
unstable seams. This diversity of wrongness is not a bug. It is one of the main ways the world
remains historically thick and contested rather than collapsible into one omniscient viewpoint.
Council language should remain survivably partial rather than total.
Development-only diagnostics must never be copied directly onto public surfaces.
D.2 How reveals should feel
Core reveals should feel like reinterpretation rather than replacement. When the planetary
experiment becomes legible, players should feel that earlier play suddenly has more depth, not
that it becomes invalid. When the Learning Delve begins to emerge, players should feel that the
world is becoming stranger without losing the ordinary pressures they already know. When
meta-layer destabilization appears, it should widen the horizon rather than flatten the prior
layers into solved exposition. Cookbook reveals should feel dangerous, unstable, and culturally
risky. Council contact should feel momentous, delayed, and only partially survivable. Both should
remain scarce enough that community memory can crystallize around them.
Never turn hidden layers into routine checklist content.
Never deliver major truth through sterile exposition detached from run consequences.
E. Player-experience architecture
E.1 Discovery layers
Discovery Layers exist because the architecture has become deep enough that players need
guided accretion of literacy. A new player should not be asked to understand civilization schools,
doctrine weather, Archive archaeology, or Cookbook diffusion on day one. The game should first
teach bodily truth: move, carry, extract, hesitate, rescue, witness, survive. Only after bodily truth
is familiar should social ambiguity, branch differentiation, and crawl identity become explicit.
Archive archaeology, world-map disagreement, civilization schools, and anti-Protocol traces
should arrive later as earned reinterpretive layers.
Early onboarding should favor acted examples over textual explanation.
Public wording on early surfaces should stay Layer-1 safe and behaviorally useful.
E.2 Skill bands and retention
Retention in The Delve Protocol should come from three main sources: mastery, discovery, and
mythology. Mastery means learning movement, branch timing, burden transfer, and social
staging. Discovery means learning what the cave, the culture, and the world-map are trying to tell
you. Mythology means returning because prior runs still matter and because your actions now
enter public memory. Player legend identity helps connect all three. A player who becomes
known as a rescuer, a scandal magnet, or a collapse survivor should feel that the world
remembers them socially, not just numerically.
Do not confuse grind with mastery.
Do not confuse lore unlocks with discovery. Discovery should usually change how future play
is interpreted. F. Mechanics and social deduction
F.1 Social-handling vocabulary Artifacts and burdens are among the richest social signal generators in the
game. The architecture should preserve explicit vocabulary for offering, refusing, handoff, solo-carry, burden transfer, public drop, loaded refusal, concealment, staged display, opportunistic theft, protective escort, and memorial burial. This vocabulary matters because it lets derivative designers think clearly about what behaviors the game can surface and how the Archive might later read them. Whenever new item types are added, designers should ask not only 'what does this do mechanically?' but also 'what kinds of offering, refusing, escorting, or concealment behaviors does this create?'  Items are social objects first and tools second.  Meaningful burden handling should always have interpretive consequences. F.2 Traversal mastery Traversal mastery in this game should feel like embodied literacy, not twitch-only optimization. Examples include burden juggling under witness pressure, route timing under environmental collapse, coordinated rescue under containment drift, and pressure-aware splitting or regrouping. High-skill play should make players more expressive, not less legible. Because signal decay is a long-horizon risk, the architecture must preserve situations where optimal play still creates socially interpretable variation.  Do not design mastery systems that reduce all good players to one statistically neutral behavior profile. G. Movement economy and pressure G.1 Why movement matters so much The whole world depends on players having multiple reasons to move. If movement collapses into a straight line toward an artifact and then to the exit, the labyrinth stops being socially expressive. The movement economy therefore has to produce overlapping motivations— curiosity, suspicion, fear, opportunity, obligation, memory, and witness pressure—so that players split, regroup, investigate, avoid, and stage themselves in meaningful ways. The strongest movement systems are the ones that change how players read each other. Signals, rumor fragments, echo chambers, witness zones, and cultural landmarks are especially valuable because they create ambiguity without forcing explanation.  Movement incentives should often conflict rather than align.  The cave should sometimes make the safe route socially risky and the risky route culturally tempting. G.2 Psychological pressure Dread, urgency, relief, scandal tension, ritual gravity, and anti-Protocol unease should be authored as part of run feel. A cave that is mechanically varied but emotionally flat will not produce strong memory. Conversely, emotional variety without mechanical grounding becomes vague atmosphere. The constitution therefore treats emotional pressure as the experiential layer of the physical pressure model.
Not every run should have the same emotional register.
A meaningful fraction of runs should stay cold or sparse so louder runs retain force.
H. Generation, branches, and room semantics
H.1 Room semantics as social architecture
Room semantics should be authored as social and interpretive architecture, not only hazard
containers. A burden corridor is not just a narrow room with movement friction; it is a place
where carrying becomes visible and escort decisions become legible. A witness hall is not just a
wide room; it is a public pressure instrument. A ritual threshold is not just a decorative doorway;
it is a place culture will load with meaning over time. Designers should think in terms of how
spaces create readings: standoff spaces, rescue chambers, memorial wells, witness corridors,
containment pockets, and temptation routes.
Good rooms say something about behavior before they say something about combat or loot.
Branch identity should be readable in route character, witness pressure, and burden
handling, not just art style.
H.2 Branch drift
Branch drift should be slow, legible, and historically meaningful. A branch should not mutate so
fast that myths and schools cannot form around it. But it must mutate enough that communities
cannot fully solve it forever. Drift can appear through route harshness, rescue bias, witness
density, artifact reputation, relay tendency, ritual heat, and public fascination.
Branch evolution should feel like history, not balance patching.
I. Crawl network and relay doctrine
I.1 Relay as definitive architecture
Relay nodes must be treated as core architecture from this point onward. They are not bonus
complexity. They are one of the main ways the world achieves planetary scale, social
unpredictability, and cultural recombination. Relay events should feel like meaningful re-routing
of history, not just clever queue management. Because relay nodes are so important, every future
networking and generation design should ask how a given choice will behave once relay
recombination is live at scale.
Relay nodes should support merge, split, redirect, exposure, and mixed-pressure
recomposition states.
Relay should intensify cultural exchange and myth propagation, not dilute identity.
I.2 Cultural geography
Landmarks matter because they tie route memory to world memory. A betrayal chamber, a
famous rescue site, an origin room for a cursed lineage, or a memorial well becomes a movement
attractor in later runs. This is one of the main ways the world begins to feel inhabited by memory
rather than by content.
Landmarks should sometimes stay live and sometimes cool into archaeology.
J. Protocol states and population adaptation
J.1 Behavioral envelopes
Expedition, Fracture, Intimate, and Exposure Protocols should remain more than labels.
Expedition should mean broad witness pressure and public spectacle. Fracture should mean split
meaning, selective recombination, and branch commitment cost. Intimate should mean pair and
burden dynamics becoming culturally sharp. Exposure should mean isolation itself becomes the
pressure condition and the Delve's pattern-awareness is felt more strongly. The protocol-state
envelope should affect environmental pressure scaling, inhabitant mix, challenge framing,
Archive emphasis, and broadcast emphasis.
Do not let protocol-state flavor collapse into cosmetic labels.
Keep the reduced live labels in code, but preserve the richer endgame behavioral envelope in
the canon. K. Lattice and inhabitants separation
K.1 Why the separation is sacred
If runtime inhabitants become direct puppets of the interpretive minds, the world collapses into a
legible puppet show and player interpretation shrinks. The constitution therefore protects the
hard separation: minds author conditions and motifs; inhabitants express pressure within those
conditions through the deterministic owner path. This separation preserves fairness, readability,
and the sense that the world is layered rather than theatrically controlled.
Predators, Echoes, Protocol Agents, and environmental entities belong to the run/network
ecology.
Examiner, Trickster, Archivist, Cartographer, and Warden belong to the pre-run interpretive
lattice. L. Cookbook system restoration
L.1 Holders, marginalia, and the hidden network
The Cookbook must be restored in the master constitution as more than a pacing rule. It includes
fragments, holders, marginalia, hidden continuity, anti-Protocol descent, and collapse doctrine.
Holders are not a faction. They are a hidden network of players who recognize ways to break or
redirect protocol expectations. Marginalia are not flavor text. They are a method by which
forbidden literacy accumulates. Cookbook continuity should spread through anomaly-linked
discoveries, post-core echoes, repeated impossible inversions, relay-era rumor, and rare Archive
tensions that do not fit public explanations.
The Cookbook network must remain hidden, unstable, and socially dangerous.
Do not flatten the Cookbook into a clean codex or perk track.
L.2 Collapse conditions
The experiment-collapse event exists as a constitutional possibility so the endgame has true
structural stakes. It should remain extraordinarily rare and should require alignment between
anti-Protocol reads, fragments, holders, and conditions. Its purpose is not to provide an ordinary
win state but to keep the possibility of structural instability real.
Collapse should be remembered as a world event, not just a private ending.
M. Archive, dissent, and archaeology
M.1 The Archive as institution, not oracle
The Archive should feel like an institution with memory, style, confidence, and bias—not a
neutral data dump. That means it should preserve dissent, lag, hot interpretations, weak
readings, and archaeology. It can be official without feeling infallible. Archive archaeology should
allow players to compare eras, read item lineages, inspect branch evolution, and revisit famous
crawls through reconstruction rather than exact replay.
Archive disagreement should remain controlled and readable.
Do not turn archaeology into a giant museum that replaces return pressure.
N. Broadcast, fascination, and public culture
N.1 Broadcast as cultural amplifier
Broadcast should not become a parallel spectator product. It should intensify the same culture
already carried by the Archive, the shell, and world memory. When a crawl becomes infamous or
a rescue becomes legendary, broadcast should increase fascination, not detach the event from the
world that made it meaningful. Fascination weather should make some branches, crawls,
artifacts, and schools temporarily hotter without reducing the world to trend chasing.
Broadcast should feel serious, eerie, and culturally charged.
O. Civilization, crews, and legends
O.1 Player legend identity
Player legend identity is a major retention layer because it gives personal narrative ownership
without flattening the game into stat classes. A rescuer, betrayer, relic hunter, collapse survivor,
witness chaser, or crew anchor should feel socially real because the world remembers patterns,
not because the game assigns a permanent class. Similarly, crews and schools must be
remembered as active cultural forms. Rescue crews, cartographer schools, artifact custodians,
ritualists, scandal crews, and anti-Protocol readers should all be possible long-horizon identities.
Legends should be inferred from behavior rather than selected in a menu.
P. DelveMind and world health
P.1 Entropy and diagnostics
The project is strong enough now that it must be treated like a living world from the design stage.
World health diagnostics are therefore mandatory, not optional. Archive overload, myth
saturation, route-meta dominance, civilization siloing, ritual coercion, artifact prestige collapse,
and Cookbook elitism must all be watchable conditions with culturally legible responses. Those
responses should feel historical rather than patch-like: cooling, burial, retirement, branch drift,
myth extinction, Archive de-emphasis, and inter-crawl exchange.
DelveMind may shape protocol-space deeply, but must preserve human uncertainty and
ordinary expedition life. Q. Epochs, mutation, and map telemetry
Q.1 Mutation must be discoverable Epochs and mutation only matter if players can actually notice
them. That means world-map telemetry, landmarks, branch climates, Archive tone, and public fascination need to change in ways that are legible but not omniscient. Collapsed passages, revived routes, changed branch marks, or artifact-source recontextualization are all good examples because they are discoverable and historically expressive. The map should function as a partial official memory, a ritual object, a rumor engine, and a contested cultural text. Its power comes from structured incompleteness.  Distinguish personal cartography, public cartography, Archive-confirmed routes, and Archive-suspected routes.  Keep anomaly clusters, confidence gradients, and fascination weather visible without making the map total truth. R. Testing and roadmap discipline R.1 Why future-phase tests matter now A system can be canonically required before it is live in code. That means the testing chapter must preserve future-phase categories even before those systems are implemented. Relay routing, large-population protocol-state behavior, broad inhabitant ecology, Cookbook fragment determinism, and relay merge/recombination are examples of future-phase tests that should remain visible in the constitution now so implementation waves do not forget them later.  Proof gates should remain deterministic, wording-safe, and truth-boundary aware. R.2 Roadmap honesty The roadmap must continue to distinguish current implementation risk from future architecture desire. The master constitution is not a permission slip to pretend everything is already built. It is a map of what the project must eventually become if it is to stay itself across years of development. S. Short-Horizon Movement Triggers and the Immediate Why-Move- Now Loop 35.1 Why movement still needed a final pass The movement economy is one of the most important upgrades in the late architecture, but movement systems only achieve their full purpose when they create immediate, felt reasons to move now rather than abstract reasons to move in general. The cave must never reduce to a distant artifact objective plus extraction corridor. Players need overlapping micro-motives that create moment-to-moment relocation pressure even when the macro-objective is understood. Short-horizon movement triggers are therefore the run-scale instruments that make the broader movement economy actually playable. They are the place where curiosity, suspicion, witness pressure, rescue obligation, environmental urgency, and rumor collision become behavior instead of theory. 35.2 Core trigger families Every run should be capable of activating several trigger families at once. Information triggers include partial signals, contradictory pings, resonance flares, echo snippets, or temporary chart updates that imply something is happening but never fully define it. Environmental triggers include rising water, shifting darkness, contamination spread, structural instability, visibility inversion, route collapse warning, or moving safe ground. Social triggers include public witness exposure, being seen carrying a burden through a witness corridor, another player being observed entering a culturally charged landmark, or relay rumor suggesting a familiar crew is nearby. Resource triggers include rescue tools, temporary burden relief, branch-specific traversal aids, unstable caches, or one-time path access openings. Temporal triggers include late- run convergence, extraction countdown pressure, dissipating signal windows, or anomaly phases that appear only briefly. 35.3 Trigger design law Triggers should not merely create movement; they should create readings. A good trigger changes how players interpret each other while they move. A player who detours into a memorial well during low-pressure time reads differently from a player who does it while a burden carrier is lagging behind. A player who chooses a rumor corridor instead of an extraction lane under witness pressure reads differently from a player who avoids all contested information. These distinctions are the reason triggers must be authored with social consequence in mind, not only navigational consequence. The final rule is simple: if a movement trigger never changes what other players think happened, it is probably too weak for The Delve Protocol.

36. Fun-First Environmental Pressure Doctrine 36.1 Environmental pressure as socially interpretable crisis Environmental pressure should never become a separate survival game layered on top of the expedition. The purpose of flooding, darkness, contamination, collapse, noise attraction, and containment pulses is not to create generic hardship. The purpose is to force socially interpretable choices: who gets escorted, who gets left behind, who refuses a transfer, who risks the public route, who runs dark, who drags a burden through a witness hall, who chooses ritual over speed, and who changes their story when the environment changes under them. 36.2 The physical expression of pressure families Each pressure family should have visible, tactile environmental forms. Trial pressure may appear as harder route commitment, collapsing footing, harsher burden friction, or rooms that punish hesitation. Deception pressure may appear as contradictory wayfinding, visibility distortion, false branch confidence, or temporary environmental evidence that can be misread. Discovery pressure may create optional side access, resonance windows, hidden chambers, or route asymmetry that rewards curiosity. Memory pressure may thicken echo recurrence, bring back old inscriptions, make cultural landmarks active again, or create rooms where the past seems present. Risk pressure should increase volatility and make powerful choices harder to price. Containment pressure should redirect, close, isolate, reroute, and challenge assumptions about safe retreat. 36.3 Environmental fairness law The game must never feel unfairly capricious. Environmental pressure is allowed to stress players, divide players, and force tragic choice. It is not allowed to feel like arbitrary punishment detached from the authored run identity. Players should be able to say, after the fact, that a run was harsh, deceptive, oppressive, narrow, or unstable—and those judgments should correspond to what the environment actually expressed. That is why the pressure system must remain bounded, deterministic, and legible through symptoms rather than through explicit UI exposition.
37. Ritual Ecology, Ritual Lifecycle, and Sacred Locations
37.1 Rituals as living systems rather than flavor Rituals are no longer just worldbuilding texture. They are a core social system that binds runs, crawls, schools, myths, and broadcast culture together. Players create rituals through repeated action under pressure: burial practices, handoff oaths, relay confessions, memorial descents, oath routes, refusal customs, shrine pauses, burden witness practices, pre-extraction invocations, and anti-Protocol inversions. The architecture should therefore treat ritual as living behavior that can emerge, spread, institutionalize, corrupt, split, revive, or die. 37.2 Ritual lifecycle Every ritual should be intelligible across a lifecycle. Emergence occurs when repeated acts under similar pressure become recognized by more than one crew or school. Adoption occurs when that act becomes recommended, expected, or mythically meaningful. Institutionalization occurs when the Archive, broadcast culture, or powerful schools begin treating the ritual as proper behavior. Corruption occurs when the ritual becomes coercive, performative, strategically exploitable, or detached from its original meaning. Revival occurs when a dead or corrupted ritual is reinterpreted under new branch conditions, during a civilization shift, or because a famous crawl makes it meaningful again. This lifecycle gives ritual a historical shape rather than reducing it to static atmosphere. 37.3 Sacred locations Some locations should accumulate a sacred or notorious gravity beyond ordinary room semantics. These include burden altars, confession chambers, witness halls, memorial wells, relay shrines, rescue thresholds, scandal sites, oath corridors, and impossible-return routes. These places should alter behavior even when they do not alter mechanics. Players should move differently, speak differently, hesitate differently, and judge differently when those rooms are active. Sacred locations should become part of both the map and the Archive, appearing as remembered places that carry cultural charge far beyond their geometry. 38. Broadcast Culture as Pressure Amplifier, Not Just Memory Surface 38.1 Broadcast changes behavior before it changes history Broadcast culture is already a core civilizational layer, but the final architecture needs to make one thing explicit: public fascination changes play before it changes memory. When a branch is hot, when an artifact lineage is culturally loaded, when a relay scandal is fresh, or when a crawl is under public fascination, players should feel that attention. They should fear being seen with certain burdens, seek witness when trying to look clean, avoid culturally charged routes when they do not want to be judged, and sometimes choose reckless symbolic action precisely because the world is watching.
38.2 Fascination cycles as social weather World fascination should behave like cultural
weather. Some weeks or eras the public is obsessed with rescues. Some periods are saturated with betrayal panic. Some branch reputations become fashionable, sacred, suspect, or exhausted. These cycles should influence Archive emphasis, map heat, relay rumor density, witness pressure, and the moral tone through which actions are interpreted. The goal is not to make players chase trend meters. The goal is to make them feel that the world has a mood and that mood reaches into the crawl. 38.3 Broadcast discipline Broadcast must remain serious, eerie, culturally charged, and selective. It is not a clip factory. It is not streamer-first spectacle. It is the public pressure layer through which the world frames expeditions as stories, scandals, sacrifices, and warnings. Any future broadcast expansion should therefore ask how it changes pressure, reputation, and mythogenesis—not just how it increases visibility. 39. Crew Identity, Crew Lifecycle, and Expedition Schools 39.1 Crew identity vs player legend vs civilization school The architecture now contains player legend identity and civilization schools, but crews are the social unit where most players will first feel culture become real. A crew is not just a party and not yet a civilization school. It is a recurring social body with remembered tendencies, trust shape, route habits, burden customs, rescue expectations, and scandal potential. A player may belong to a school without always running with the same crew. A crew may inherit a school’s philosophy while developing its own distinct style. 39.2 Crew lifecycle Crews should have lifecycles just as crawls and rituals do. A crew can emerge, stabilize, become famous, fragment, cool, or become archaeological. A crew becomes memorable not only because of win rate or artifact count but because of repeated behavioral signatures: always rescuing late, always splitting on rumor, always preserving lineage over speed, always taking public routes under scandal pressure, always returning to the same relay shrines, always refusing cursed burdens. The Archive should remember crews in ways proportional to their cultural effect, not just their activity count. 39.3 Expedition schools Schools are larger and slower than crews. They represent philosophies of descent: heroic rescue doctrine, strict artifact preservation, anti-Protocol exploration, survival-first pragmatism, witness-cleansing, cartographic rigor, oath literalism, Archive skepticism, and more. Schools should affect how players read crawls, how rituals spread, how maps are authored, and how certain artifacts or branches are publicly discussed. Schools should compete culturally without monopolizing legitimacy. The architecture must preserve plural legitimacy so no single school becomes the only civilized way to play.
40. Relay Emotional Doctrine, Relay Culture, and Relay Scandal
40.1 Relay as planetary-scale social architecture Relay nodes are already definitive architecture in the endgame design, but the final book must go further and define what relay feels like. Relay is not a hidden queue system. It is a historical and emotional turning point. It is where crews merge, split, misread each other, encounter strangers, inherit rumors, lose control over continuity, and feel the larger planetary scale of the crawl network. Relay should produce a specific emotional mixture: anxiety, possibility, contamination of certainty, social recomposition, and the sense that one’s descent has entered a bigger history. 40.2 Relay culture Over time, relay nodes should develop their own micro-cultures. Some relays are feared because they expose burdens publicly. Some are revered because famous rescues happened there. Some are known for betrayal scandals. Some are used by cartographers, some by Archive-minded schools, some by anti-Protocol trespassers. Relay culture should therefore influence route planning and social expectation before a run begins. 40.3 Relay scandal Relay scandals are major cultural events because relays are where histories collide. A relay scandal might involve a stolen lineage artifact, a false rescue ritual, a mass misdirection event, a betrayal under public witness, a collapse caused by overused route consensus, or a failed anti- Protocol convergence. These events should become world myths, map scars, Archive controversies, and school arguments. Relay is one of the best engines the architecture has for civilization-scale storytelling and should be treated that way. 41. Path Entropy, Discovery Drift, and Route Meta Self-Correction 41.1 Path entropy Perfect architecture must defend itself against route optimization. Path entropy is the law that frequently overused routes gradually become less stable, less rewarding, more watched, more inhabited, more culturally loaded, or more dangerous. This does not mean balance patching. It means the labyrinth remembers and responds. The purpose is to prevent any permanent single best path from dominating long enough to flatten play. 41.2 Discovery drift Discovery drift is the inverse. Neglected paths, forgotten rooms, cooled branches, and archaeologically ignored chambers should become newly interesting over time. Signals intensify there. Echoes gather there. Artifacts migrate there. Cultural meaning can revive there. Together, path entropy and discovery drift make the world self-correcting without betraying determinism.
41.3 Route-meta diagnostics The world-health layer should specifically monitor route-meta
dominance. It should not wait until routes are fully solved. Rising path entropy, branch monotony, witness redundancy, or collapsing rumor diversity should all count as early warnings that the crawl network is becoming too predictable. 42. Artifact Reputation, Burden Signatures, and Social Handling Expansion 42.1 Artifact reputation as living culture Artifacts should not only have lineage; they should acquire reputations. A lineage can be cursed, heroic, infamous, sacred, scandal-marked, or exhausted. These reputations should directly change player behavior. A famous artifact entering a crawl should alter trust, route selection, witness pressure, and burden transfer ethics before any explicit mechanic resolves. 42.2 Burden signatures Artifacts should also physically and socially change the shape of runs. They may emit noise, glow under darkness, attract inhabitants, distort witness pressure, reveal hidden paths, suppress safe routes, or trigger dormant ritual sites. These are burden signatures: persistent tendencies through which an artifact changes traversal rather than merely changing score or lore. 42.3 Handling vocabulary The final constitution should preserve the full handling vocabulary from the item ecology docs: offering, refusal, handoff, solo-carry, burden transfer, public drop, loaded refusal, staged exchange, hidden custody, ritualized relinquishing, and scandal carry. This vocabulary is one of the most important bridges between artifacts and social deduction, and future derivative docs must not collapse it into generic inventory interactions. 43. Behavioral Signatures, Contradiction Events, and Deduction Depth 43.1 Behavioral signatures Players and crews should gradually develop recognizable behavioral signatures. These are not stats or hidden ratings; they are patterns the world and community come to know. Examples include rescue-first, artifact-chasing, witness-seeking, relay-opportunist, cartography-rigorous, ritualist, collapse-scarred, scandal-avoidant, and false-calm styles. Behavioral signatures deepen human reading because they give players expectations that can be confirmed, performed, inverted, or betrayed. 43.2 Contradiction events To keep deduction from collapsing into simple heuristics, the labyrinth should sometimes generate contradiction events. These are conditions where two readings seem plausible but
cannot both be true, or where the same evidence points in incompatible directions. Contradiction events push deduction beyond simple observation and force players to reason through pressure, timing, culture, and motive. 43.3 Deduction law The game must never drift toward abstract hard-proof accusation systems. Contradictions should sharpen inference, not replace it with deterministic reveal mechanics. The job of the architecture is to keep players arguing, not to let the environment solve social meaning for them. 44. Cultural Weather, Cartographic Schools, and World Map Actionability 44.1 Cultural weather World fascination cycles are now part of the architecture, but they should be weighted as cultural weather: the global mood that changes how branches, artifacts, crawls, and player legends are read. Some eras are rescue-heavy. Some are burden-paranoid. Some are artifact- obsessed. Some are Archive-skeptical. This weather should show up in map heat, broadcast emphasis, witness pressure, rumor density, and school arguments. 44.2 Cartographic schools The map is already a contested cultural text. To complete that doctrine, the architecture should explicitly preserve competing cartographic schools: practical route maps, mythological maps, rumor maps, ritual maps, anti-Protocol maps, Archive-confirmed maps, and witness maps. These are not aesthetic variants; they are different epistemic strategies for reading the labyrinth. 44.3 Actionable world map doctrine The player-facing map must do more than symbolize uncertainty. It should inform actual decisions while preserving doubt. It must clearly distinguish personal charting, public charting, Archive-confirmed paths, Archive-suspected paths, anomaly clusters, rumor heat, fascination heat, disputed sectors, and lost zones. Players should be able to make meaningful pre-run decisions from the map without ever receiving total strategic truth. 45. Environmental Entities and Deep Ecology 45.1 Fourth ecology layer The inhabitant model already includes predators, echoes, and protocol agents. The final ecology should also include environmental entities: fungal intelligence, shifting stone systems, contamination swarms, pressure storms, echo-thick weather, branch-corruption blooms, and other non-animal pressure forms. These are not just hazards. They are semi-living systems that reshape movement, meaning, and branch history.
45.2 Deep ecology law The purpose of environmental entities is to make the labyrinth feel alive without turning it into a monster
zoo. Environmental entities should be understood as ecological pressure forms whose presence changes route logic, witness conditions, burden viability, map confidence, and school myth. They extend the crawl ecology rather than distracting from it. 45.3 Integration with pressure and epochs Environmental entities should be pressure-responsive and epoch-sensitive. A memory-heavy era may produce echo storms. A contamination epoch may create fungal schools or map panic. A containment-heavy phase may awaken structural intelligences that redirect crews through old relay scars. This lets ecology, history, and civilization reinforce each other. 46. Infamy, Witness Pressure, and Personal Myth Gravity 46.1 Infamy mechanics Player legend identity becomes much stronger when the world remembers not only greatness but also dangerous social gravity. Infamy is the pressure created when a player is publicly known for a style, scandal, refusal, or burden pattern. Infamy should not function like ranked punishment. It should function like cultural gravity: people anticipate things when that player appears. 46.2 Witness pressure When a player, crew, branch, crawl, or artifact is culturally hot, witness pressure should rise. Players should feel the difference between carrying a nameless burden and carrying a famous lineage artifact while the public is watching that branch. This is one of the key bridges between broadcast, Archive, and minute-to-minute play. 46.3 Personal myth gravity Some players should become mythically heavy. Their presence in a crawl changes expectation, fear, aspiration, or suspicion. The architecture should allow that gravity to matter culturally while still protecting fairness and plural legitimacy. 47. Archaeological Expeditions, Reconstruction Runs, and Historical Play 47.1 Archaeological expeditions The civilization layer becomes much richer once players are allowed to engage history actively. Archaeological expeditions are descents driven by historical questions: can we reconstruct the Red Descent, find the origin chamber of a scandal artifact, verify whether a ritual actually began in the branch that claims it, or test whether a relay betrayal story was real or compressed by Archive bias?
47.2 Reconstruction runs Reconstruction runs are not literal
replays. They are historically informed descents through contemporary labyrinth conditions, guided by old map fragments, Archive contradictions, relay rumor, and artifact lineage traces. They should feel like historical inquiry under pressure, not museum tours. 47.3 History as gameplay This is one of the strongest final upgrades to the architecture. It turns the Archive from a passive memory surface into a generator of future play. The world becomes a place where the past is playable, contestable, and never fully settled. 48. Cultural Festivals, Memory Echo Events, and Micro-Drama Engine 48.1 Cultural festivals Some world-scale moments should not be crises or scandals. The civilization layer also needs commemorative pressure. Examples include Red Descent anniversaries, Artifact Remembrance Week, relay pilgrimages, branch mourning days, Archive opening ceremonies, and school- foundation rites. These are not generic seasonal events. They are historically loaded opportunities for culture to renew itself. 48.2 Memory echo events The labyrinth should sometimes replay fragments of historical crawls through inscriptions, sounds, route ghosts, or resonance phenomena. These events should encourage archaeology, alter path choice, and make the world feel layered rather than current-only. 48.3 Micro-drama engine Not every memorable moment should depend on a full myth cycle. The cave should also create small dramatic events: an artifact falling into a chasm, a route collapse separating a burden carrier, an inhabitant migration burst, a witness inversion that turns safety into suspicion, a relay reroute that changes who leaves together. These micro-dramas are the fuel of post-run storytelling and should be treated as deliberate design targets rather than lucky accidents. 49. Hardest Stress Tests, Impossible Player, and Anti-Optimization Guarantees 49.1 The impossible-player test The impossible player is mechanically perfect, socially manipulative, strategically informed, culturally literate, and historically experienced. The architecture should still prevent this player from permanently dominating the world. Path entropy, discovery drift, contradiction events, relay recombination, civilization pluralism, burden signatures, environmental entities, and distributed causality all exist partly to make permanent total mastery impossible.
49.2 The 100-year test Across a century of play the world must preserve archaeology without becoming museum-like, renew myths without deleting history, allow schools to form without freezing legitimacy, and mutate routes without feeling like patch
churn. Epochs, myth extinction, stratified Archive memory, cultural weather, and world-health diagnostics must therefore all remain active and mutually aware. 49.3 The anti-optimization guarantee The constitution cannot guarantee that players will never optimize. It can guarantee that optimization never closes the world. Any stable route, school, artifact interpretation, or social heuristic must be vulnerable to drift, contradiction, mutation, reputational inversion, or cultural cooling. That is how the game remains alive. 50. 70. Artifact Loss, Burial, and Unrecoverability Doctrine
70.1 Why unrecoverability must be treated as real
If an artifact falls into a spike pit, collapses into sealed geometry, is entombed behind a containment shift, vanishes into a void chamber, or is otherwise rendered unreachable, the event must remain valid run truth. The architecture must not quietly respawn the artifact in a way that erases the event. An unrecoverable artifact is not a bug-shaped hole in the design; it is a meaningful state that can become scandal, burial, sacrifice, ritual anchor, map scar, archaeology hook, or branch reputation event.
70.2 Artifact loss states
The world should distinguish among at least these loss states: dropped-but-reachable, temporarily inaccessible, branch-sealed, entombed, shattered, lineage-shadowed, ritually buried, collapse-lost, and myth-lost. Each state has different downstream consequences for Archive treatment, future archaeology, map telemetry, artifact lineage, and public memory.
70.3 Anti-exploit rule
Players may never use artifact dumping as a trivial burden bypass. Systems must ensure that deliberate loss carries real consequence through some combination of branch stigma, lineage damage, world fascination, witness suspicion, future archaeology cost, or reduced artifact-legitimacy value. The constitution must preserve room for sacrifice and emergency abandonment without rewarding casual throwaway optimization.
70.4 Archaeology and lineage continuation
An unrecoverable artifact does not automatically end its lineage. A lineage may continue as a lost object, a branch scar, a rumor node, a ritualized burial site, an archaeological target, or a reconstructed myth object. The Archive and world map should be able to remember not only possession but also absence, burial, and unresolved disappearance.
70.5 Support-doc inheritance
ITEMS_AND_SYNERGIES remains the local-detail authority for artifact handling vocabulary and lineage logic. ARCHIVE_SYSTEM remains the local-detail authority for how loss becomes shorthand, myth, or archaeology. World-map and branch-memory surfaces may deepen the cultural expression of unrecoverable loss only within the privacy and confidence rules already defined in the constitution.
71. Manifested Intelligence Contact, Violence, and Succession Doctrine
71.1 Three categories of contact
The architecture must distinguish clearly between ordinary NPC-like entities, manifestation-bearing entities, and high-order deep-contact manifestations. Ordinary entities live on the in-run pressure layer. Manifestation-bearing entities are local shells carrying some expression of a lattice motive or mind-fragment. High-order deep-contact manifestations are rare protocol-contact events that approach Council or governance-facing intensity.
71.2 Violence against ordinary entities
Attacking an ordinary pressure entity changes run conditions, witness interpretation, branch memory, and Archive framing like any other meaningful act. The event remains in run truth and may intensify scandal, trespass, or rescue interpretation.
71.3 Violence against manifestation-bearing entities
Most manifestation-bearing entities should not equate one-for-one with a total lattice mind. Striking or destroying a manifested shell usually collapses the local manifestation, interrupts that contact channel, and may trigger withdrawal, containment response, echo residue, doctrine weather shift, or public myth. The underlying lattice mind generally persists unless the encounter has been constitutionally escalated to a high-order contact state.
71.4 Rare succession rule
At rare deep-contact thresholds, manifest-shell destruction may cool, retire, or force succession within the active mind ecology. This must remain scarce and world-weighted. It is not routine combat. The point is to let certain extraordinary acts alter motive emphasis or visible manifestation ecology without turning the game into a 'kill the gods' action structure.
71.5 Hard prohibitions
Mind manifestations must not become ordinary boss fights. Runtime violence must never imply that the player has gained engine-space sovereignty. A manifested shell may die; the protocol-space ecology behind it remains governed by constitutional scarcity, succession, and DelveMind oversight.
71.6 Support-doc inheritance
AI_INHABITANTS remains the clearest local authority on lattice-vs-inhabitant separation and on the distinction between pressure entities and pre-run minds.
72. Diegetic Communication and Ominous Guidance Doctrine
72.1 Why communication now matters
The architecture already supports signals, inscriptions, echoes, Archive language, broadcast, and manifestation. What needed to be made explicit is that NPCs, protocol-facing entities, and manifested minds may sometimes speak to players directly or semi-directly. This is safe and desirable when the communication intensifies pressure, care, ambiguity, or guidance without replacing player judgment.
72.2 Allowed communication channels
Permissible channels include inscriptions, echo playback, relay announcements, ritual-room utterances, protocol-agent warnings, branch signage drift, artifact whispers, post-run shell-safe framing, rare on-screen mind messages, NPC dialogue, Council-mask speech, and environmentally embedded textual or audio cues. All channels must remain diegetically flavored and bounded by tone and truth-layer doctrine.
72.3 Ominous guidance rule
Helpful communication should usually be partial, eerie, motive-bearing, and open to suspicion. It may suggest routes, warn against trust, imply ritual significance, or frame an artifact decision, but it must not become a perfect tactical companion. The player should feel aided, judged, manipulated, or watched, not escorted by an infallible quest marker.
72.4 Frequency and intensity limits
Direct communication should remain scarce enough to preserve wonder. It should intensify in high-pressure, high-motive, or deep-contact conditions, and cool in ordinary periods. The architecture should preserve long spans where communication remains indirect, environmental, or ambiguous.
72.5 Example communication shapes
Examples that fit the constitution: 'Left route. Not because it is safe.' 'Do not hand it to the quiet one.' 'This hall remembers liars.' 'Carry it yourself, if you want the room to stay open.' These lines guide pressure interpretation while preserving agency and mystery.
72.6 What communication must never do
Communication must never reveal hidden truth too early, disclose protected runtime facts, flatten ambiguity, or make player choice feel obsolete. It must not turn the game into a chatty AI companion structure. It exists to intensify interpretation, not to solve the cave.
72.7 Communication test doctrine
Every communication surface must be tested for truth-boundary safety, tone safety, and player-agency preservation.
73. Contaminated Reference and Drifted Allusion Doctrine
73.1 Why references require discipline
Sparse traces of old-world culture, degraded media, or pop-cultural residue can fit the project's DCC-like weirdness, but only if transformed enough to preserve the world's seriousness, archaeology, and eerie tone. Direct meme insertion or topical joking would break gravity and historical thickness.
73.2 Allowed forms
Permissible forms include corrupted slogans, half-decayed lyrics, impossible brand fragments, ritualized nicknames for artifacts, anomalous archive misclassification, strange branch graffiti, and echo fragments that feel displaced, damaged, or centuries out of context.
73.3 Forbidden forms
Do not insert present-tense topical references as winks to the audience. Do not use pop culture as disposable joke delivery. Do not allow direct references to dominate branch identity, ritual culture, or Archive gravity.
73.4 Use cases
The best use cases are anti-Protocol marginalia, echo anomalies, artifact nicknames, branch inscriptions, relay rumor, or degraded old-world residues that feel uncanny rather than comedic. References should usually be partial enough that they invite uncertain recognition rather than certainty.
73.5 Tone rule
The world may occasionally be darkly funny, but it must not become jokey. Any reference drift must feel like contamination, residue, or misremembered civilization, not contemporary fandom inserted whole.
74. Movement Motive Composition Law
Every run must activate at least three movement motives drawn from different families: one informational motive, one environmental or temporal motive, and one social or burden motive. This rule exists so the labyrinth never collapses into a single straight-line objective structure. Example family sets include signal chase + flooding urgency + burden escort; rumor contradiction + route collapse + witness pressure; or cultural landmark pull + contamination drift + public offering risk.
75. Relay Player-Feel Contract
Relay nodes must always change at least one of the following: who you are with, what you know, what route is viable, how your burden is interpreted, or how publicly legible you are. Relay is not merely smart routing. It is a civilization engine, a scandal engine, and a cultural mixer. If a relay leaves all five dimensions unchanged, it has failed constitutionally even if it was structurally valid.
76. Anti-Survival-Minigame Environmental Clause
Environmental systems may be intense, but they must never become a self-sufficient survival game that drowns out traversal-first deduction. Flooding, darkness, oxygen scarcity, contamination, visibility inversion, noise attraction, and collapse should primarily make rescue, refusal, burden handling, witness pressure, and route choice more legible. They exist to intensify social interpretation, not to replace it.
77. Crew Archive Recording Rule
The Archive must record crews through behavior patterns rather than static roster permanence. What matters is rescue style, burden custom, relay habit, ritual habit, scandal type, collapse response, and witness posture. Crew identity should emerge through repeated action and interpretation, not through guild-like menu objects detached from play.
78. Map Privacy and Confidence Rule
78.1 Privacy
No map surface may reveal hidden-run truth through aggregate certainty. Public heat, route confidence, anomaly clusters, and rumor density must remain partial enough that map telemetry never becomes an omniscient truth oracle.
78.2 Confidence display
Every map surface must clearly display its epistemic class: personal, public, official, suspected, disputed, archaeological, cooled, or lost. This lets players know not only what the map says, but what sort of statement it is making.
78.3 Actionability
The map should shape pre-run expectation, route anxiety, and cultural interpretation without replacing on-the-ground judgment. Confidence labels must therefore inform but not solve.
79. Broadcast Spectacle Drift Metric
The world-health dashboard must explicitly track whether broadcast emphasis is drifting toward novelty over meaning, clips over cultural context, scandal over complexity, or visibility over archaeology. This metric should be reviewed whenever public-facing content begins rewarding spectacle at the expense of social ambiguity, branch gravity, or historical depth.
80. Cookbook Anti-Monopoly Rule
No single holder, crew, school, or hidden network may monopolize Cookbook progression for the world. Cookbook meaning must remain fragmentary, rumor-bearing, diffusible, partially misreadable, and contestable. The deeper the Cookbook becomes, the more important diffusion, false seams, imitation failures, and contradictory marginalia become. This protects the game from elite capture.

84. Player Agency Visibility Doctrine
Players should usually be able to tell what they changed, what they triggered, what they failed to prevent, and what the world is now likely to remember about them. They do not need perfect causal omniscience, but they do need enough legibility to feel consequential rather than absorbed into abstract mystery. Agency visibility is especially important in Archive framing, broadcast aftermath, and crew/civilization memory.
85. Fair Surprise Rule
The labyrinth may surprise players, but surprise must emerge from pressure interaction, historical residue, symbolic misread, route consequence, inhabitant response, contradiction, or motive drift. Surprise must not come from arbitrary reversal of learned logic without warning structure. The world may be uncanny; it must not feel like it cheated.
86. Cultural Memory Proportionality Rule
Not every incident deserves equal memorial weight. Archive and world memory should remember in proportion to significance, repetition, scandal, ritualization, cultural adoption, and symbolic resonance. This keeps public memory believable and prevents the world from turning every minor event into false history.
87. UI Silence Protection Rule
When the correct constitutional response is uncertainty, the interface must be allowed to show ambiguity, missingness, disagreement, cool-down, delay, or no official answer. UI is not permitted to flatten the world into over-clarity merely because implementation convenience prefers explicitness.
88. False Mastery Guard
The architecture must periodically challenge confident heuristics through contradiction events, drifted branch meaning, changed artifact reputation, witness inversion, relay recomposition, cooled-myth reinterpretation, and other bounded disruptions. The purpose is not to punish mastery, but to keep mastery alive by preventing it from fossilizing into solved certainty.
89. Negative Space Doctrine
The labyrinth must preserve low-signal rooms, quiet branches, under-explained ruins, culturally empty corridors, cooled spaces between major meanings, and stretches of traversal where the cave is not loudly performing significance. This contrast makes mythic places more powerful and keeps the world believable rather than over-authored.
90. Recovery Doctrine
Players, crews, schools, and branches must be able to recover from scandal, collapse, failed rituals, botched rescues, bad reads, cooled myths, and reputational harm. Recovery should not erase history; it should produce new history. Worlds that only punish become static. Worlds that permit recovery become historical.

103. Artifacts as Experimental Instruments
Artifacts are not merely burdens, temptations, or prestige objects. They are experimental instruments through which the Protocol studies trust, prestige hunger, sacrifice, burden transfer, rescue ethics, witness behavior, ritualization, concealment, scandal, and myth formation. This doctrine sharpens a truth already present across the item, mechanics, and world-bible layers: artifact meaning is never exhausted by a static item definition. Every major lineage should be designed as a behavior-shaping instrument first and a collectible object second.
In practical terms, an artifact line may be tuned to produce different experimental readings. A lineage may intensify escort behavior by making carriers slow, loud, luminous, or spatially conspicuous. Another lineage may intensify suspicion by behaving cleanly in isolation but erratically under witness pressure. Another may deepen rescue ethics by forcing a choice between preserving the object, preserving a teammate, or preserving the legitimacy of a ritual route. These distinctions should appear not only in flavor text or post-run summaries, but in how players move, hesitate, transfer, refuse, escort, conceal, and publicly display the object during the run.
Artifact design should always answer: what human pressure does this object create?
Artifact lineages should generate route consequences, social readings, and later Archive shorthand, not just item text.
A lineage should be allowed to change moral tone across eras without changing basic owner-tree authority.
The architecture therefore treats artifacts as part of the experimental variable engine. They can modulate witness density, prestige incentive, rescue burden, social interpretation density, ritual emergence, public visibility of choice, and route certainty distortion. Future implementation plans should keep this experimental-instrument framing explicit so artifacts never drift toward ordinary RPG loot or abstract score objects.
104. Selective Mechanical Imports and Rejections
The constitution now explicitly states how outside dungeon-crawler or systemic-game mechanics may be inherited. This rule exists because the project has reached a maturity level where inspiration drift becomes a greater danger than system scarcity. External mechanics are allowed only when they are reinterpreted through Delve logic: traversal-first social reading, pressure-shaped decision making, and historically meaningful return.
Allowed mechanical imports are not imported whole; they are translated. Non-Euclidean or hyperbolic topology may appear only as a rare branch or anomaly condition when it supports misdirection, path doubt, and memory conflict rather than becoming the normal geometry of the world. Environmental survival meters such as oxygen, darkness, or flooding may appear only when they intensify burden handling, rescue tradeoffs, and witness pressure rather than becoming a separate survival game. Death residue may persist when it supports archaeology, memorialization, or lineage history rather than permanent class progression. Anti-stall systems may devour, collapse, seal, or contaminate rooms when doing so forces movement and social interpretation rather than simple speedrunning.
Explicitly rejected imports include any mechanic that would reposition the game as something it is not. A permanent human game-master role, a combat-interface gimmick that replaces embodied play, standard monster-transformation progression, direct save-scumming as a default tool, full-grid party-crawler ontology, or direct quest-companion AI navigation are all constitutionally unsafe unless drastically transformed.
Adopt the pressure truth behind an external mechanic, not the mechanic in its genre-native form.
If an import shifts meaning away from traversal, burden, rescue, witness, or return, it is almost certainly wrong for this world.
Imported systems must be justified in derivative specs with a note explaining why Delve logic needs them and what genre drift they are explicitly avoiding.
105. Integrated Bible, Cookbook, and Council Clarification
The final constitution already preserves Bible legitimacy, Cookbook danger, mutual dependence, and Council rarity, but this integrated clarification locks their relationship more tightly so future implementation cannot flatten it. Bible-aligned life remains the dignified, sufficient public body of the world. It preserves the habits, rituals, institutions, and interpretive structures that make the labyrinth inhabitable. Cookbook literacy remains a fragmentary, risky, transformative trespass literacy. It destabilizes, rereads, or exploits the very structures Bible culture preserves. Council contact remains a rare recontextualization event through which the world may partially reveal its governance-facing intelligence without becoming a settings menu.
The most important law here is proportionality. Bible is not fake. Cookbook is not superior. Council is not administrative UI. Deep-contact players may influence motive emphasis, world weather, or future pressure framing, but they may not invalidate the ordinary dignity of sanctioned expedition life. Likewise, sanctioned players are not excluded from meaning simply because they never become Cookbook holders. This keeps the truth layers plural rather than hierarchical in a simplistic way.
Bible preserves legitimacy and survivable coherence.
Cookbook preserves dangerous reinterpretation and transformative trespass.
Council preserves governance-facing recontextualization without operational overexposure.
These three layers must therefore be designed as interdependent social realities rather than an upgrade ladder. Public life must remain culturally sufficient, hidden literacy must remain fragmentary, and Council contact must remain rare enough that whole communities can build myths around it.
